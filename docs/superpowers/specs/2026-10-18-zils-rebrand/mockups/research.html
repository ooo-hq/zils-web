03Approach

What we’re training.

A model that assigns probabilities to a set of possible answers. Each experiment tests a specific decision, such as which action to take next.

1

Start with a base model.

The support, flight, and chess studies use the published JevK5 4B weights. This unchanged model is the baseline for measuring what training adds.

2

Teach one task with an adapter.

A LoRA adapter is a small set of learned weights added to the model. The base weights stay frozen while the adapter learns from examples for that task.

3

Test on separate examples.

Compare models on the same held-out cases. Measure correctness, probability quality, and simple alternatives. Test data does not select the adapter.

04Completed comparison · 6 October 2026 · 500 ABCD test conversations

Models trained for customer support.

Given a customer conversation, its previous actions, and a workflow guide, choose the next action from 30 options. ABCD is a public dataset of support role-play conversations; the recorded action is the test answer.

Our trained model matched that answer 79.2% of the time, compared with 70.6% for TypeSafe JEV 1.13.0. Both received the same inputs, instructions, and choices.

What the comparison holds fixed

The Zils adapter trained on 1,024 separate conversations and was selected on a different development set. Its weights were frozen before the final test cases were prepared.

We then called TypeSafe’s hosted JEV API on those same test inputs. The Zils scores reuse verified predictions from the completed training study. No test answers were sent to either model, and no model or prompt was tuned during this comparison.

Our trained model got 72 cases right that JEV missed. JEV got 29 right that our trained model missed.

05Chess specialization · 7 October 2026 UTC

Chess: find the move that ends the game.

Choose an immediate checkmate from a supplied list of legal checking moves. A correct answer ends the game in one move; this does not test playing a whole game.

A fresh chess adapter selected a checkmating move in 260 of 512 held-out positions. Accuracy improved by +7.81 percentage points over the model before training and +7.23 over TypeSafe JEV.

All legal checking moves were offered. Every move that immediately checkmates received credit. This measures one-move choices among legal checks. Deterministic chess software solves 100% of these positions; the study tests specialization on a new task. One training seed, one recipe, no deployment.

06Completed experiment · 5 October 2026 UTC

Flight risk: historical rates still scored best.

Estimate whether a flight will arrive at least 15 minutes late, using scheduled information only. On 1,024 later test flights, the trained adapter reduced probability error by 6.5% versus the base. A simple historical-rate baseline still had the best measured score.

Both models had their confidence scores adjusted on separate February data. All three were tested on the same March flights. Scale 0 to 0.20.

What training improved

The adapter produced better probability estimates and ranked delay risk more accurately. Its risk-ranking score (ROC-AUC) rose from 0.516 to 0.640. The measured probability-error improvement over the base was supported by a 95% interval that stayed above zero.

Where it fell short

Historical rates scored slightly better; the difference was uncertain. The experiment required the adapter to beat both references. It did not meet that bar, and no model was deployed from this run.

Why 80.37% accuracy is not the headline
Most flights were not late. At a 50% probability cutoff, the adapter predicted “not late” for every flight and flagged none of the 201 late arrivals. The improvement was in its risk estimates, not in useful delay alerts at that threshold. An alert threshold needs its own validation.
07Base tier · status as of 17 October 2026

A competition for the base model itself.

Customer adapters train on a pinned base model. Since 17 October 2026 that base is d3-mini 4.5B (below); before that it was H2O Lightning 4B v1.2.3. The base tier is the competition that decides when that base changes. Miners submit a full-weight replacement for the pinned base of a text profile. Validators score it on a sealed holdout against the incumbent, and a candidate is promoted only if it passes every step below.

StepRule
Sealed scoreValidators score the submitted weights on a sealed holdout, against the incumbent.
EligibilitySkill is positive, accuracy is not below the incumbent’s, and Brier improves by at least 0.01 with a paired-bootstrap 95% lower bound above zero.
Round weightEligible candidates share the round’s weight in proportion to skill.
ConfirmationThe best eligible candidate is re-scored on a second, disjoint sealed draw at least 12 hours later and must stay eligible.
Regression suiteBefore promotion, a fixed suite of reference adapter jobs is retrained on the incumbent and on the candidate. The candidate must be no worse than 0.005 Brier on every job and better on at least one.
ChannelAdvancesWho gets it
latestAdvances on each confirmed winner.Opt-in, per job.
stableAdvances at most quarterly, to a base that held latest through a full window without rollback.The default for new customer jobs.

New customer jobs default to stable; latest is opt-in per job. Existing releases keep serving on their base.

Status, 17 October 2026
Implemented in the subnet repository. Open admission is off by default, and no public round has run. Round one’s sealed holdout has 1,458 teacher-authored cases in six families. The incumbent, stock H2O Lightning 4B v1.2.3, scores Brier 0.184 and 87.7% accuracy on it. d3-mini’s confirmation draw did not confirm a winner, but d3-mini became the base by decision on 17 October, so both channels now point at it and stock d3-mini is the incumbent for round two.

10Metric guide

Read the numbers.

Being correct and knowing how likely you are to be correct are different properties. We report both.

Accuracy

The fraction of decisions that match the test answer. On the support test, 396 / 500 = 79.2%. It measures the recorded next action, not the success of an entire conversation.

Percentage points vs fewer mistakes

79.2% − 70.6% = an 8.6-point accuracy gain. The same result is 43 fewer errors out of JEV’s 147, or 29.3% fewer mistakes. These describe the same comparison using different denominators.

Confidence and coverage

Confidence here is the probability assigned to the chosen answer. Coverage is the share of cases above a probability threshold. High accuracy on a small confident subset does not mean high accuracy on all cases.

Calibration

Among predictions assigned about 90% probability, roughly 90% should be correct. A model can improve accuracy while remaining overconfident. ECE summarizes the gap between confidence and accuracy across bins.

Mining, validation, and the recorded testnet round are on the Bittensor page. All results on this page are recorded experiments. The study reports define their scope, artifacts, and reproduction limits.
